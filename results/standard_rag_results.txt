----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Вопрос:  What is the formal definition of a limit?
  Эталон:  A limit uses the epsilon-delta definition: for every epsilon > 0 there exists delta > 0 such that |x - c| < delta implies |f(x) - L| < epsilon.

  Ответ RAG / RAG Answer:
        The formal epsilon-delta definition states that for every epsilon
    greater than zero there exists a delta greater than zero such that whenever
    the distance between x and the limit point is less than delta, the distance
    between f(x) and L is less than epsilon. A limit describes the value that a
    function approaches as the input approaches a particular point. Both rules
    are derived from the definition of the derivative using limits. The
    derivative of a function f(x) is defined as the limit of the difference
    quotient: f'(x) = lim(h->0) [f(x+h) - f(x)] / h. Limits are foundational to
    all of calculus.

  Эталон:  Part 1: if F(x) = integral from a to x of f(t)dt, then F'(x) = f(x). Part 2: integral from a to b of f(x)dx = F(b) - F(a), where F is an antiderivative of f.

    calculus. This theorem is fundamental in fluid mechanics, electromagnetism,
    and heat transfer. The Divergence Theorem (Gauss's Theorem) relates the flux
    of a vector field through a closed surface to the volume integral of the
    divergence over the enclosed region: integral_S F .

  Вопрос #4 [Фактический / Factual]
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Вопрос:  What is the divergence of a vector field?
  Эталон:  The divergence of F = (P,Q,R) is dP/dx + dQ/dy + dR/dz. It measures the rate at which the field spreads out from a point. Zero divergence means the field is solenoidal.

  Ответ RAG / RAG Answer:
        The divergence of a vector field F = (P, Q, R) is a scalar: div(F) =
    dP/dx + dQ/dy + dR/dz. The Divergence Theorem (Gauss's Theorem) relates the
    flux of a vector field through a closed surface to the volume integral of
    the divergence over the enclosed region: integral_S F . The curl of a vector
    field F = (P, Q, R) is a vector: curl(F) = (dR/dy - dQ/dz, dP/dz - dR/dx,
    dQ/dx - dP/dy). It measures the rate at which the field spreads out from a
    point. A positive divergence indicates a source, while negative divergence
    indicates a sink.

  Вопрос:  How are derivatives and integrals related?
  Эталон:  The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus connects them: differentiation and integration are inverse processes. The integral of a derivative recovers the original function (up to a constant).

  Ответ RAG / RAG Answer:
        Partial derivatives and multiple integrals extend single-variable
    calculus to higher dimensions. The gradient generalizes the derivative, the
    divergence and curl extend differential analysis to vector fields, and
    double and triple integrals generalize single integrals. A triple integral
    extends double integrals to three dimensions, computing quantities like mass
    and volume over a three-dimensional region. Triple integrals can be
    evaluated in Cartesian, cylindrical, or spherical coordinates, with
    appropriate Jacobian determinants for coordinate transformations. Related
    rates problems involve finding the rate of change of one quantity in terms
    of the rate of change of another.

  Вопрос:  How does the Chain Rule relate to integration by substitution?
  Эталон:  Integration by substitution (u-substitution) is the reverse of the Chain Rule. If the integrand has the form f(g(x))*g'(x), substituting u=g(x) simplifies the integral.

  Ответ RAG / RAG Answer:
        Integration by substitution, also known as u-substitution, is the
    reverse of the Chain Rule. The Chain Rule states that the derivative of a
    composite function f(g(x)) equals f'(g(x)) multiplied by g'(x). The Chain
    Rule extends naturally to compositions of three or more functions. This rule
    is essential for differentiating nested functions. The Product Rule states
    that the derivative of a product of two functions u(x) and v(x) is u'(x)v(x)
    + u(x)v'(x).

  Вопрос:  Why does a Taylor series converge only within a radius of convergence?
  Эталон:  A power series converges where the terms become small enough to sum to a finite value. The radius of convergence R is determined by the ratio or root test applied to the coefficients.

  Ответ RAG / RAG Answer:
        Within the radius of convergence, a power series can be differentiated
    and integrated term by term. Each power series has a radius of convergence R
    such that the series converges for |x-a| < R and diverges for |x-a| > R. A
    Taylor series represents a function as an infinite sum of terms calculated
    from the values of its derivatives at a single point. The Taylor series of
    f(x) around x = a is given by sum [f^(n)(a)/n!] * (x-a)^n. A power series is
    sigma from n=0 to infinity of c_n * (x-a)^n.

  Эталон:  The Jacobian accounts for how the coordinate transformation stretches or compresses area/volume elements. Without it, the integral would not correctly measure the transformed region.

  Ответ RAG / RAG Answer:

    integrals. Partial derivatives and multiple integrals extend single-variable
    calculus to higher dimensions. Triple integrals can be evaluated in
    Cartesian, cylindrical, or spherical coordinates, with appropriate Jacobian
    determinants for coordinate transformations. For a transformation from (u,v)
    to (x,y), the Jacobian J = d(x,y)/d(u,v) = (dx/du)(dy/dv) - (dx/dv)(dy/du).
    In polar coordinates, the Jacobian is r, so dA = r dr d(theta).

  Вопрос:  How is the second derivative used to classify critical points?
  Эталон:  At a critical point where f'(c)=0: if f''(c) > 0 the function is concave up so it is a local minimum; if f''(c) < 0 the function is concave down so it is a local maximum.

  Ответ RAG / RAG Answer:
        The First Derivative Test uses sign changes of f'(x) to classify
    critical points. Critical points occur where f'(x) = 0 or f'(x) is
    undefined. The Second Derivative Test uses f''(x): if f''(c) > 0 the point
    is a local minimum, if f''(c) < 0 it is a local maximum. The second
    derivative f''(x) represents the rate of change of the rate of change, which
    geometrically describes the concavity of the curve. Points where concavity
    changes are called inflection points.

    revolution, and physical quantities like work, center of mass, and moments
    of inertia. Mixed partial derivatives f_xy and f_yx are equal for functions
    with continuous second-order partial derivatives, by Clairaut's theorem
    (also called Schwarz's theorem). The gradient generalizes the derivative,

    double and triple integrals generalize single integrals.
